# Founding 20 Program
## Professional Mastery Community — مجتمع السيطرة المهنية

**Document status:** Proposed operating design for the first pilot cohort  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 12 November 2026  
**Cohort target:** 20 selected participants  
**Participant fee:** Free for the entire first cohort  
**Recommended duration:** 12 weeks, followed by 30-, 90-, and 180-day outcome follow-ups

> This document defines a programme that PMC can operate and measure. It is not an announcement that applications are open, a promise of employment, or evidence that partners or mentors have committed.

## 1. Purpose and definition of success

Founding 20 is a selective, no-fee professional development pilot designed to prove that PMC can help a small group turn professional potential into demonstrable work, stronger professional evidence, and better access to opportunities.

The cohort is not successful merely because 20 people attend sessions. It is successful when PMC can show, participant by participant and in aggregate:

1. A clear baseline and an individual development plan.
2. Demonstrated improvement in selected professional competencies.
3. Practical work products or projects that can be reviewed.
4. Improved professional evidence, such as a CV, LinkedIn profile, portfolio, case study, or documented contribution.
5. Better access to relevant opportunities, with interviews and employment outcomes reported only when verified.
6. A repeatable operating playbook, with actual effort, cost, capacity, and participant feedback recorded.

The first cohort remains free. Sponsorship, future premium services, or corporate funding must not introduce a payment requirement for this cohort.

## 2. Pilot assumptions and boundaries

The following are planning assumptions to approve before recruitment:

| Item | Proposed pilot setting |
|---|---|
| Cohort size | 20 participants |
| Duration | 12 weeks |
| Follow-up | 30, 90, and 180 days after programme end |
| Mentorship | One mentor or specialist for approximately 4–5 participants, subject to availability |
| Selection | Competitive, rubric-based, reviewed by at least two people |
| Delivery | Mostly remote and low-cost; practical work and small-group sessions |
| Fee | No fee for selected participants |
| Certificate | Issued only after the evidence-based graduation review; attendance alone is insufficient |

PMC's current public website and content are especially developed around healthcare professionals, while the wider brand covers professional development, innovation, research, data, and AI. The pilot should recruit where the current content, tools, and available mentors can provide genuine value. Broadening into sectors with no delivery expertise should follow demonstrated capacity, not precede it.

## 3. Candidate identification and recruitment

Use a mixed sourcing model so the cohort is not composed only of people already close to the founders.

**Candidate sources**

- Public call through the PMC website and approved social channels.
- Referrals from existing community members and professional contacts.
- University career centres, alumni networks, student and graduate societies.
- Volunteer specialists, mentors, and professional associations.
- Employer and institutional referrals, without allowing a referring organisation to reserve places or control selection.

**Recruitment message**

Describe the programme accurately: free, selective, practical, evidence-based, time-bounded, and with no guaranteed job, promotion, interview, or certificate. Explain the expected time commitment, evidence requirements, selection process, use of information, and how a candidate can withdraw.

**Application material**

Collect only information needed to assess fit and operate the programme:

- Name and one contact method, retained in the existing identity/CRM layer.
- Current professional stage and broad field.
- One or two development goals.
- A short description of a problem the candidate wants to solve.
- A work sample or example of initiative, where available; provide an accessible alternative for candidates without a formal portfolio.
- Availability and preferred support.
- Separate, optional permission for future marketing or public storytelling.

Do not ask for national ID, health information, religion, family details, or other sensitive information as routine selection fields.

## 4. Eligibility and selection framework

### 4.1 Eligibility gate

Assess eligibility as pass, hold for clarification, or not eligible. Proposed gates:

- The application is sufficiently complete to assess.
- The candidate can identify a practical professional development need.
- The candidate accepts the programme's conduct, evidence, privacy, and participation expectations.
- The candidate can agree a realistic development plan for the pilot period.
- The candidate's goals can be supported by PMC's actual content, tools, mentors, or partner capacity.

Eligibility is not a popularity test. Social following, university prestige, employer brand, and polished self-promotion are not substitutes for evidence of potential.

### 4.2 Merit rubric — 100 points

Two reviewers independently score the application and work sample. Scores are then calibrated against the same rubric.

| Criterion | Weight | What reviewers look for |
|---|---:|---|
| Initiative and follow-through | 20 | Evidence of taking responsibility, completing work, or acting on feedback |
| Learning agility and problem solving | 20 | Ability to learn, reason, adapt, and explain how a problem could be approached |
| Practical evidence and execution | 20 | A work sample, project, contribution, or credible example of applied effort |
| Clarity of goals and opportunity path | 15 | A specific development goal and a plausible route from learning to value |
| Readiness and commitment | 15 | Realistic availability, openness to feedback, and ability to complete agreed outputs |
| Collaboration and communication | 10 | Clear communication, respectful collaboration, and willingness to contribute to peers |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Reviewers should record evidence for each score, not impressions alone. A structured interview or short work sample may be used where the written application does not provide enough evidence. Accommodations and alternative formats should be available where practical.

**Decision rule:** select the highest-merit candidates who meet the approved quality threshold and can be supported by the available programme capacity. If fewer than 20 meet the threshold, extend recruitment or run a smaller cohort rather than lower the bar. Maintain a reserve list only with the candidate's permission. Record conflicts of interest and require an independent second review for any affected decision.

Selection data and reviewer notes are restricted operational records, not public content.

## 5. Participant activation and individual development plans

Each selected participant receives a pseudonymous programme reference for measurement. Identity and contact details remain in the restricted identity/CRM layer.

During onboarding, the programme lead and participant agree an Individual Development Plan (IDP) containing:

- A baseline for 3–5 relevant competencies.
- One primary professional objective and up to two supporting objectives.
- The participant's current evidence and constraints.
- Two or three concrete deliverables.
- A weekly action plan and review dates.
- A named mentor or specialist contact, if available.
- Opportunity pathways relevant to the participant's goals.
- A definition of what evidence would demonstrate progress.

The IDP is reviewed at the midpoint and updated only with a recorded reason. Do not impose the same goals on all 20 participants.

## 6. Professional skills and practical learning

The programme should combine common foundations with individual pathways. The common foundation can include:

- Career direction and opportunity mapping.
- Evidence of professional value and achievement documentation.
- Problem solving, communication, collaboration, and professional reliability.
- CV, LinkedIn, and portfolio improvement.
- Practical use of relevant digital tools, AI, analytics, and research methods.
- Interview preparation and professional networking.
- Ethics, privacy, responsible AI, and professional conduct.

Participants then choose a focused development pathway with the programme team. Do not claim that all participants have developed a skill merely because a workshop covered it. Skill development requires a baseline, a final assessment, and evidence.

## 7. Practical assignments and projects

Each participant must complete applied work, not just consume content.

**Suggested deliverables**

1. A problem statement and a short opportunity map.
2. A project brief with scope, intended user or beneficiary, and success criteria.
3. At least one prototype, analysis, case study, process improvement, research summary, portfolio artifact, or other relevant work product.
4. A short evidence record explaining the participant's contribution, decisions, and results.
5. An updated professional profile or equivalent evidence pack.

Projects may be individual or small-group. Group work must identify each person's contribution. A project is not counted as delivered until a reviewer has checked it against agreed acceptance criteria.

## 8. Mentorship and specialist engagement

Create a small network of volunteer specialists and mentors rather than a large, loosely managed mentor directory.

- Target approximately 4–5 participants per mentor where capacity allows.
- Agree mentor scope, availability, conduct, confidentiality, and response expectations in advance.
- Use structured 30–45 minute sessions or office hours, with a short action record.
- Allow specialist clinics for CV/LinkedIn review, research, analytics, AI, entrepreneurship, or sector-specific topics.
- Record only the fact and type of engagement, next action, and participant feedback. Do not record private personal conversations as analytics.
- Escalate conflicts, inappropriate contact, or safeguarding concerns to the programme lead.

Mentors advise; they do not promise employment, guarantee referrals, or make selection and graduation decisions for participants they directly assess without independent review.

## 9. Recruitment and opportunity pathways

PMC should create access, not promise results.

Potential pathways include:

- Employer briefings and problem statements.
- Curated job, internship, project, and freelance opportunities.
- CV and interview clinics.
- Mock interviews with structured feedback.
- Introductions to organisations where there is a genuine role or project fit.
- Participant showcases with explicit opt-in before any public profile, portfolio, or project is shared.
- Guidance on applications and follow-up.

Record the stages separately: opportunity surfaced, shared with participant, participant applied, interview invitation received, offer received, and outcome accepted or declined. A referral or job link is not an interview or employment outcome.

## 10. Recommended 12-week delivery cycle

| Phase | Weeks | Main work | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activate and baseline | 0–1 | Orientation, baseline, IDP, goals | Baseline rubric and signed IDP acknowledgement |
| Build foundations | 2–3 | Career direction, skills, tools, achievement evidence | Skills exercises and initial evidence log |
| Apply and create | 4–7 | Practical project, specialist input, mentor reviews | Project brief, milestones, feedback, draft output |
| Package value | 8–9 | CV, LinkedIn, portfolio, case-study refinement | Before/after review and evidence pack |
| Connect to opportunities | 10–11 | Employer clinics, interviews, showcase preparation | Opportunity records and participant next steps |
| Assess and close | 12 | Final review, presentation, feedback, next plan | Final rubric, project acceptance, exit survey |

After week 12, conduct follow-ups at 30, 90, and 180 days. Follow-up participation is voluntary and should use clear, purpose-specific consent.

## 11. Graduation standard

Graduation is an evidence-based decision. Attendance and activity are inputs, not proof of achievement.

A participant is eligible for graduation when the review panel can confirm:

1. At least one practical project or professional deliverable has been submitted and assessed against its brief.
2. The participant demonstrates progress in at least two IDP competencies, using the same anchored rubric at baseline and final review.
3. The participant can explain their own contribution and provide a professional evidence artifact, such as an improved CV, LinkedIn profile, portfolio, or approved equivalent.
4. The participant presents a credible next-step opportunity plan.
5. The participant has met the programme's conduct and evidence-integrity requirements.

Use a four-level competency rubric: 1 = not yet demonstrated; 2 = emerging with substantial support; 3 = independently demonstrated to the expected standard; 4 = strong, repeatable performance that can be explained and evidenced. The panel should document the evidence for each rating.

Employment is not required for graduation because it is partly outside the programme's control. Employment outcomes remain an important follow-up measure. Participants who do not yet meet the standard receive a clear development note and may be recorded as completed-but-not-graduated or invited to an approved remediation path. Do not issue a graduation claim based solely on attendance.

## 12. Governance and responsibilities

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| PMC owner / sponsor | Approves scope, policy, budget, cohort announcement, and final operating decisions |
| Programme lead | Owns delivery, participant communication, IDPs, schedule, risk log, and reporting |
| Selection panel | Scores applications independently and documents decisions |
| Mentor / specialist | Provides scoped guidance and reviews agreed deliverables |
| Data steward / authorized operator | Maintains approved records, access, quality, retention, and export controls |
| Impact reviewer | Checks evidence, definitions, denominators, and claims independently of delivery where feasible |
| Participant | Owns their goals, contributions, evidence accuracy, and consent choices |

For the pilot, one person may hold more than one operational role where necessary, but selection and graduation decisions should receive a second review to reduce bias.

## 13. Launch gates

Do not announce that applications are open until the owner approves:

- Programme dates, eligibility, selection threshold, delivery capacity, and named programme lead.
- Application wording, privacy notice, consent records, retention, and contact rules.
- Data residency and cross-border transfer review for the current Supabase deployment.
- The application route and end-to-end save/withdrawal tests.
- Reviewer, mentor, and participant operating procedures.
- A working impact dashboard or an approved interim reporting process.
